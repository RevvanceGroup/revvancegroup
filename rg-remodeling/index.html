Questions

Straight Answers

How much does a roof replacement cost in Pittsburgh?

As a rough range, asphalt shingle replacements on typical Pittsburgh homes run from around $10,000 to $20,000, depending on the size and pitch of the roof, how many layers come off, and the condition of the decking underneath. We walk the roof, then give you a written, itemized estimate for free. The number we put on paper is the number you pay.

How long does a roof replacement take?

Most homes are torn off and finished in a single day. Our customers say it in their reviews: the crew shows up early, stays until it is done, and runs a magnet sweep so no nails are left in the yard. Bigger or steeper roofs can take a second day, and we tell you that before we start.

Do you help with storm damage and insurance claims?

Yes. After wind or hail, we inspect the roof for free, document the damage with photos, and give you a straight answer on whether it is a repair or a claim. If it is a claim, we provide the documentation your insurance company needs and do the work once it is approved.

What shingles do you install?

We are an Owens Corning Preferred Contractor and install Owens Corning architectural shingle systems, which lets us offer extended manufacturer warranty options on qualifying roofs. If you have another product in mind, we will give you an honest comparison before you decide.

Do you really do remodeling too?

Yes, and it is half the name. Roofing fills the fair-weather months, and through the winter the same crew builds kitchens, bathrooms, and interior projects. One recent customer put it simply: they finished the kitchen, flawless work, ten out of ten. Booking a winter remodel in the fall is the easiest way to skip the spring rush.

What areas do you serve?

We are based in Castle Shannon and work across Pittsburgh's South Hills: Bethel Park, Mt. Lebanon, Upper St. Clair, Baldwin, Brentwood, Whitehall, Dormont, plus city neighborhoods like Beechview, Brookline, Carrick, and nearby boroughs including Green Tree, West Mifflin, and Homestead.
