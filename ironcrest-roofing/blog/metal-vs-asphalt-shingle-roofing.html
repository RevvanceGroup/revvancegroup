Metal vs asphalt shingle roofing in the Central Valley

Updated March 10, 2026 · By Ironcrest Roofing Co. · 6 min read

Asphalt shingles and metal roofing are both excellent choices for our climate, and we install plenty of each. The right pick depends on how long you plan to stay, your budget, and how much you care about energy savings. Here is an honest side by side, without pushing you toward the pricier option.

Upfront cost

Asphalt shingles win on price. A quality architectural shingle roof costs noticeably less to install than metal, which makes it the practical choice for many families and for homes you may sell within a decade. Metal costs more upfront, often roughly double, but that buys a longer life and lower running costs.

Lifespan

  • Asphalt shingles: about 20 to 30 years in Valley heat
  • Standing seam metal: about 40 to 70 years

If you plan to stay in your home for the long haul, a metal roof may be the last roof you ever buy. If you expect to move, the lower upfront cost of asphalt often makes more sense.

Energy and summer comfort

This is where metal pulls ahead in Stockton. Metal roofing with a reflective, cool-roof finish bounces sunlight away instead of soaking it up, which can ease the load on your air conditioner during our long, hot summers. Asphalt has improved with cool-roof shingle options too, but metal still tends to lead on reflectivity.

For a home that bakes every July and August, a reflective metal roof can pay back part of its higher price through lower cooling bills over the years.

Looks and noise

Asphalt shingles come in a wide range of colors and blend in with most neighborhoods. Modern metal comes in many profiles and colors too, including styles that mimic shingles or tile, so it no longer means a barn-style look. As for rain noise, properly installed metal over solid decking and underlayment is much quieter than people expect.

So which should you choose?

Choose asphalt shingles if you want the lowest upfront cost, a proven product, and a roof that may outlast your time in the home. Choose metal if you plan to stay long term, want the longest lifespan, and care about cutting summer cooling costs. Both, installed correctly, will protect your home for decades.
