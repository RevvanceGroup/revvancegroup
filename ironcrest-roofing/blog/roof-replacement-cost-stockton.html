How much does a roof replacement cost in Stockton in 2026?

Updated April 2, 2026 · By Ironcrest Roofing Co. · 6 min read

It is the first question almost everyone asks, and the honest answer is that it depends. Two houses on the same street can have very different roofing bills. Still, you deserve real numbers, so below are the factors that move the price and the typical ranges we see for an average single family home in Stockton and the surrounding county.

What actually drives the price

  • Roof size. Roofing is priced per square, which equals 100 square feet. A bigger roof simply needs more material and labor.
  • Material. Basic three-tab shingles cost the least, architectural shingles a bit more, and metal or tile considerably more.
  • Pitch and complexity. Steep roofs and ones with lots of valleys, hips, dormers, and skylights take more time and care.
  • Tear-off and layers. Removing old roofing, especially multiple layers, adds labor and disposal cost.
  • Decking repairs. If we find rotted plywood underneath, replacing it protects your new roof but adds to the total.
  • Permits and details. Permits, new flashing, vents, and gutters all factor in.

Typical price ranges in our area

For a common single story home of roughly 1,800 to 2,200 square feet, here are realistic installed ranges in 2026. Your exact number depends on the factors above, so treat these as a starting point, not a quote.

  • Architectural asphalt shingles: about $9,000 to $18,000
  • Standing seam metal: about $18,000 to $40,000
  • Concrete or clay tile: about $20,000 to $45,000
The cheapest bid is rarely the best value. A slightly higher price that includes full tear-off, new underlayment, proper ventilation, and a real warranty will almost always cost you less over the life of the roof.

Why the lowest quote can cost you more

When one bid comes in far below the others, it usually means something was left out: going over the old shingles instead of tearing off, skipping new flashing, thin underlayment, or a labor warranty measured in months instead of years. Those shortcuts show up later as leaks and a roof that ages early in our heat.

Financing makes it manageable

A roof is a large, often unplanned expense. That is why we offer financing with low monthly payments and 0% options for those who qualify, so you can protect your home now and pay over time instead of all at once.
