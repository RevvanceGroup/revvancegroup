5 signs it is time to replace your roof in Stockton

Updated May 18, 2026 · By Ironcrest Roofing Co. · 5 min read

Most roofs do not fail all at once. They wear out slowly, then a single hot summer or winter storm pushes them over the edge and you end up with a stain spreading across the ceiling. The good news is that a roof almost always warns you first. Here are five signs Stockton homeowners should watch for, and what each one usually means.

1. Curling, cupping, or buckling shingles

Asphalt shingles are designed to lie flat. When they start curling at the edges or cupping in the middle, the asphalt has dried out and lost its flexibility. In the Central Valley, years of intense summer heat speed this up. A few curled shingles can be replaced, but when it is widespread across the roof, it is a sign the whole system is near the end of its life.

2. Granules collecting in your gutters

Those tiny sand-like granules protect the asphalt underneath from UV damage. As a roof ages, it sheds them. If you are scooping piles of granules out of your gutters or seeing dark bald patches on the shingles, the roof is losing its sun protection right when our summers demand it most.

A simple test: after a windy day, check the splash zone below your downspouts. A handful of granules now and then is normal. Cupfuls are not.

3. Daylight or stains in the attic

Head into the attic on a sunny day and look up. If you see daylight coming through the roof boards, water is getting in too. Dark streaks on the rafters, damp insulation, or a musty smell all point to an active or past leak. Catching this early can mean the difference between a repair and replacing rotted decking.

  • Daylight through the deck means open gaps in the roof
  • Water stains that grow after rain mean an active leak
  • Sagging between rafters can signal trapped moisture and rot

4. The roof is simply old

A standard asphalt shingle roof in our climate lasts about 20 to 30 years. If yours is in that range and you do not have paperwork showing it was recently replaced, age alone is a good reason for an inspection. Insurance companies also start treating older roofs differently, which is worth knowing before you need to file a claim.

5. Rising energy bills and a hot upstairs

A failing roof and poor attic ventilation make your air conditioner work harder. If your upstairs has become an oven and your summer bills keep climbing, the roof and its ventilation may be part of the problem. A modern roof with proper venting, or a reflective metal system, can take real pressure off your cooling.
